================================================================================
 0. THE SHAPE OF THE WHOLE THING
================================================================================

RAG = Retrieval-Augmented Generation. Three stages:

    Home Affairs website
            |
            v
    [1] INGESTION      fetch pages -> extract text -> cut into chunks
            |          -> turn each chunk into numbers -> store in ChromaDB
            v
    [2] RETRIEVAL      your question -> numbers -> find the closest chunks
            |
            v
    [3] GENERATION     paste those chunks + your question into the LLM
            |          -> it writes an answer using ONLY that text
            v
        answer + citations

Why bother? The LLM was never trained on the current 485 rules, and rules
change. Instead of trusting its memory, we hand it today's text and say
"answer from this only". That is what makes the answer checkable.


================================================================================
 1. VOCABULARY (read this before the outputs)
================================================================================

CHUNK
    A slice of a document, roughly 1000 characters. Documents are cut up
    because you cannot paste an entire website into an LLM, and because
    searching works better on small focused pieces.

    Your settings: CHUNK_SIZE=1000, CHUNK_OVERLAP=200.

    OVERLAP means consecutive chunks share their last/first 200 characters.
    Without it, a sentence split across the boundary would be lost from both
    chunks. You can see this working in section 2 below - chunk 0 ends with
    "...Processing times" and chunk 1 begins by repeating the same lines.

EMBEDDING (or VECTOR)
    A list of 768 numbers representing the MEANING of a chunk. Produced by
    the model all-mpnet-base-v2, which runs locally on your PC - no API, no
    cost. Text with similar meaning gets similar numbers, even when the words
    are different. That is what lets "how much" find a passage that says
    "cost".

DISTANCE / RELEVANCE
    To search, your question is turned into 768 numbers the same way, then
    compared against every chunk. Closeness is measured as cosine distance
    (0 = identical meaning, 2 = opposite).

    relevance = 1 - distance,  shown as a percentage.
    Higher = closer. 70% is a strong match, 25% is weak.

VECTOR STORE (ChromaDB)
    The database holding the chunks and their 768 numbers, at
    database/vectorstore. It answers "give me the N closest chunks to this".

WHAT "GROUNDED" MEANS - AND DOES NOT MEAN
    After writing the answer, the system makes a SECOND LLM call asking:
    "is every claim in this answer supported by the source text?"

    GROUNDED means the answer matches the retrieved text.
    It does NOT mean:
      - the answer is right for your situation
      - the source page is up to date
      - the retrieval found the BEST passage
    It only catches the LLM making things up. That is why citations matter.


================================================================================
 5. HOW QUALITY IS MEASURED - hit@1, hit@3, MRR
================================================================================

The eval (scripts/eval_retrieval.py) asks 10 fixed questions. Each question
has MARKERS - words that only a genuinely correct passage contains. Example:

    question: "Is there an age limit?"
    markers : "35 years", "aged 35"

A retrieved chunk counts as a HIT if it contains any marker. The eval records
at which RANK the first hit appeared.

answerable from the corpus?

  [ok]  37 chunk(s)  Do I need a police check?
  [ok]  99 chunk(s)  How long can I stay on this visa?

  All answerable. If one said MISSING, a low score would mean "the website
  does not cover this", not "retrieval is bad" - a different problem.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 THE DEFINITIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

hit@1   Percentage of questions where the FIRST result was correct.
        The strictest measure. "Did it nail it immediately?"

hit@3   Percentage where a correct result appeared in the top 3.
        Realistic, because the system feeds 3-5 chunks to the LLM anyway -
        the answer only needs to be in the pile.

hit@5   Same idea, top 5.

MRR     Mean Reciprocal Rank. Averages 1/rank across all questions.
        rank 1 -> 1.0     rank 2 -> 0.5     rank 3 -> 0.333
        rank 4 -> 0.25    rank 5 -> 0.2     miss   -> 0

        Why bother when hit@k exists? hit@3 treats rank 1 and rank 3 as
        equally good. MRR does not - it rewards being higher up. It is one
        number capturing "how near the top, usually".

WHAT THE THREE STRATEGIES ARE

    none      Search using your question exactly as typed.

    synonyms  A hand-written jargon dictionary. "onshore" also searches
              "in Australia when you apply", "offshore" also searches
              "outside Australia". Free and instant.

    llm       Ask the LLM to rewrite your question into 3 official-sounding
              phrasings, search all of them, then merge the rankings.
              Costs one extra API call per question.

WHY EXPANSION WAS NEEDED
    Applicants say "onshore". Home Affairs writes "be in Australia when you
    apply". Same meaning, different words, and the embedding model does not
    reliably bridge that gap. Proof - the SAME chunk, same model, same index:

        "Do I need to be in Australia when I apply?"   rank 1, 62%
        "Does it matter, onshore or offshore?"         rank 3, 25%

    So the fix was not a bigger embedding model. It was asking the question
    in more than one vocabulary.

HOW THE MERGING WORKS (Reciprocal Rank Fusion)
    Each phrasing produces its own ranked list. Each chunk scores
    1 / (60 + its rank) in each list, and the scores are summed.
    A chunk that appears mid-table in ALL phrasings beats one that tops a
    single phrasing. Agreement across vocabularies is the signal.


================================================================================
 7. KNOWN WEAKNESSES (honest list)
================================================================================

1. STREAM AMBIGUITY - the biggest one
   The 485 has several streams and they cost different amounts. Both figures
   are in the corpus:
       AUD5,750.00  -> graduate-work, post-higher-education-work,
                       post-vocational-education-work
       AUD2,265.00  -> second-post-higher-education-work,
                       second-post-study-work
       AUD450       -> appears on all streams
   Ask "how much does it cost?" and the system picks one without telling you
   which stream it means. Same problem for length of stay.

2. hit@1 is 80%, so 2 questions in 10 do not rank the best passage first.
   Both still answered correctly, because the LLM gets 3-5 chunks.

3. TABLES ARE FLATTENED
   Look at the English scores in section 3: headers, then a wall of loose
   numbers. Row/column pairing is lost. The LLM reassembled it correctly this
   time, but it is guessing. This is the least trustworthy part of the system.

4. ONLY 7 PAGES
   Processing times and full fee tables live on pages the crawl did not reach.

5. NAV JUNK, mostly fixed
   "ImmiAccount / VEVO / My Tourist Refund Scheme" used to lead 59 of 232
   chunks. Now 19 of 226, and those remaining are genuine in-content mentions.

6. THE STREAMLIT WEB APP has not been tested against the current pipeline.
   Everything above was run through scripts.
